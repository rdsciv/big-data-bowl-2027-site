2027 NFL Big Data Bowl  ·  Combine tracking → NFL performance

Which way does he turn?

Scouts say a receiver opens his hips better one way. We measure that as the gap between his counterclockwise and clockwise cuts in Combine tracking, run the same detector on three seasons of NFL tracking, and test whether the weak side costs receivers separation and defensive backs coverage.

01 · Thesis

The Combine times straight lines. Football is played in turns.

In a 2019 study of NFL receivers, forty time correlated with PFF grade at r = .18, and the Combine number most associated with receiver production was the 3-cone, the drill with the most turning (r = .43 with DYAR) [2]. A stopwatch can't say which way a player turned or what it cost him. Combine tracking can: every drill attempt is recorded at 10 Hz with position, speed and heading.

Hypothesis

Prospects have a weaker turning direction that is measurable at the Combine. It persists in NFL play, where it costs separation (WR) or coverage (DB).

What we are not doing. Top speed is already solved (tracked forty peak speed matches in-game top speed at about r = 0.86), and single position-drill reps repeat at only r = 0.24–0.31, too unreliable to predict anything on their own. A within-player gap sidesteps both problems.

02 · Design

Why a within-player gap

A

It cancels the draft-slot confound

The outcome is the same player's left-break versus right-break performance. Draft capital, team, scheme and overall athleticism cancel out. What remains (field side, leverage, route type, coverage) is play-level and can be modeled.

B

The noise sits where we can measure it

Each NFL player has hundreds of cuts per direction, so the game side is reliable. The Combine side is noisy, but repeated attempts on the same side give us the noise variance directly.

C

Teams can use it week to week

Teams track every player, so in-game asymmetry is computable for any opponent: attack a corner's weak break direction, align a rookie edge to his strong side, or build a one-sided change-of-direction program.

D

A prior that the season updates

The Combine value is a prior; NFL cuts update it. We report how many NFL cuts it takes before game evidence outweighs the Combine. A free laterality signal comes along: the athlete picks which way to start the shuttle.

What the warehouse fixes

  • The tracking dis column lags the coordinate step, so path length is rebuilt from x/y.
  • Drill names drift by year (56 raw names). A crosswalk merges aliases, and a drill enters a trait only if it was run in at least two Combine years; a one-year drill is confounded with its draft class.
  • Game tracking has 8 clock gaps (six of 200 ms, two of 800 ms). Each gap starts a new segment, so no cut can span a hole.
  • Outcomes use the regular season only (249,269 player-play rows). Preseason and postseason frames stay in the frame table and out of the trait.
04 · Method

One cut detector, two domains

Step 1 · done

Warehouse

DuckDB to Parquet star schema: players, drills, games, Combine frames, game frames, regular-season player-plays.

Step 2 · done

Cuts

The same detector runs on Combine and game frames, so both sides describe one movement.

Step 3 · built

Combine prior

Empirical-Bayes level (α) and side gap (δ) per player. Code and tests written; fit next.

Step 4

NFL linkage

Hierarchical model with player × direction effects and position-specific slopes.

Step 5

Validation

Fit on the 2023–24 classes, test on 2025, against draft pick + times + NGS score.

RuleSetting
Heading3-frame centered circular mean of dir
Cut|Δ heading| ≥ 45° across 5 frames (0.5 s)
SideSign of Δ heading: counterclockwise (+) or clockwise (−)
Entry speedAt least 1.5 yd/s, otherwise slow
OverlapsApexes within 0.4 s: keep the sharpest, mark the rest suppressed
RecoveryMust regain 90% of entry speed, otherwise no regain

The 45° threshold was fixed before looking at outcomes and has not been tuned.

The Combine prior

Each player's attempts on each side give an average speed retained. We split that into a level and a side gap:

retainedi,s = αi + δi · cs + ε
cs = +1 counterclockwise, −1 clockwise · Var(ε) = σ² / ncuts
  • σ² comes from repeated attempts on the same side, so Combine noise is measured, not assumed.
  • α and δ are partially pooled (DerSimonian–Laird). δ shares one variance across positions, with separate WR and DB means.
  • A player seen on one side only updates α, not δ; his reported δ is the position prior.
  • On the NFL side the outcome is WR separation at the throw by final break direction, and DB coverage outcomes by break direction.

07 · Risks

How this could fail, and what we check first

KILL CRITERION

Split-half reliability of the Combine gap

Difference scores are less reliable than their parts. Below about 0.3, the Combine link won't survive a permutation test. The in-game asymmetry would still stand as a scouting tool, but the Combine prior would be weak.

POWER

Small groups only see big effects

With 107 WRs, 80% power at α = 0.05 detects |r| ≈ 0.27. If Combine reliability is 0.3 and game reliability 0.8, a true r of 0.4 shows up as about 0.2. Pooling WR and DB (N = 229) and the within-player outcome are the response.

BASELINE

Beat what teams already have

The comparison is draft pick + official Combine times + the NGS athleticism score already in combine_results. Beating the NFL's own composite is what team staff will care about.

FALLBACK

OL pass-set mechanics

121 offensive linemen, the least-studied group: set depth, lateral speed and re-direction time on inside moves. Less crowded, but rushers aren't tracked and a within-role control roughly halves N.

08 · Roadmap

From warehouse to Combine stage

  1. WarehouseDone
    Nine CSVs into a Parquet star schema with clock-gap QA and a regular-season outcome table.
  2. Shared cut detector and sign gateDone
    1.6M detections, 72,521 valid cuts, side split checked against drill design.
  3. Combine prior and the kill checkBuilt
    Empirical-Bayes α and δ per player (code and 34 unit tests written). Next: fit, then split-half reliability of δ.
  4. NFL outcomes by break direction
    WR separation at the throw and DB coverage outcomes, split by final break direction; hierarchical player × direction model.
  5. Out-of-class validation and write-up
    Fit on the 2023–24 classes, test on 2025, against the draft-pick + times + NGS baseline. Report the number of NFL cuts at which game evidence outweighs the Combine prior.
  6. Kaggle submission · 6 Jan 2027
    Finalists present at the 2027 NFL Scouting Combine.
09 · References

Related work

  1. Nguyen, Q. & Yurko, R. (2026). NFL step-and-turn: A generative framework for evaluating player movement in American football. arXiv:2603.17866.
    Models step length and turn angle (von Mises) with a player random effect on turn concentration. Our cut is an event-level version of their turn; their per-player concentration effect is the closest published analog to δ.
  2. Weiss, A., Thakker, R., Nayyar, J. & Schmiel, T. (2019). Combine performance vs NFL success. Wharton Moneyball Academy, Sports Analytics Student Research Journal.
    Forty time barely tracks WR grade (r = .18); the 3-cone was the Combine number most associated with WR production (r = .43 with DYAR). Small sample, but it points at turning.
  3. Burke, B. (2019). DeepQB: Deep learning with player tracking to quantify quarterback decision-making & performance. MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference.
    Judges a realized choice against what the tracked situation offered. We use the same observed-versus-expected logic, with the player's other side as the comparison.
  4. Melville, J. N. (2025). Evaluating NFL quarterback decision making through tracking data. MS thesis, Brigham Young University.
    Extends DeepQB with calibration and sharpness checks and over-expectation metrics (CPOE, YOE). A reference for validating tracking-based metrics against a baseline.
